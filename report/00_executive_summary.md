# Executive summary: Indian MSME problems that can support a startup

*Research cut-off 4 Oct 2026. ₹ values in crore/lakh. Every figure carries a source ID resolved in [15_sources.md](15_sources.md). **No primary interviews were conducted.** The validation plan is in chapter 13.*

## 1. How big is the MSME market really?

"India has 9 crore MSMEs" counts **registrations**, not businesses that can buy:

| Stage | Count | Basis |
|---|---:|---|
| Registered on Udyam (URP + Udyam Assist) | **9.16 crore** (Jul-2026) | cumulative, never purged (S06) |
| Operating non-farm establishments | **~8.0 crore** | ASUSE 2025: 7.92 crore unincorporated + incorporated (S01) |
| Use internet for business | **3.12 crore** (39.4%) | S01 |
| Commercially active, GST-filing | **~1.2–1.45 crore** | 1.67 crore GSTINs (S12), adjusted |
| Turnover ≥ ₹1.5 crore | **~20–30 lakh** | estimate, chapter 3 |
| Turnover ≥ ₹5 crore | **~6–7.5 lakh** | 8.56 lakh e-invoicing GSTINs (S14) |

- **86.6% of establishments are one-person, own-account units** with average GVA of ~₹2.5 lakh a year. They are not a SaaS market.
- **The realistic B2B market is ~8–12 lakh B2B manufacturers and distributors with ₹2–100 crore turnover**, concentrated in about 30 industrial districts: Rajkot, Ludhiana, Coimbatore, Pune, Ahmedabad, Surat, Chennai, Tiruppur, NCR and others.
- **Conflict flagged:** Udyam claims 38+ crore jobs; ASUSE counts 12.81 crore workers. Use ASUSE.

## 2. What are the biggest MSME problems?

Ranked by evidence, not anecdote:

1. **Delayed payments.**
   - **₹7.34–8.1 lakh crore** of receivables locked up (GAME 3.0, S20; Economic Survey 2025-26, S19).
   - Average payment takes **73 days** against the 45-day law (S22).
   - Only ~7% of the delayed amount has ever been filed on MSME Samadhaan (S21).
2. **Credit gap.** **₹30 lakh crore**, ~24% of demand (SIDBI-Crisil, S17). Only 41% of MSME borrowers have an active formal loan (S18).
3. **Finding and converting customers.** 70% still market traditionally (S17). MSMEs already pay **IndiaMART ₹1,569 crore** (2.28 lakh payers, ~₹69k each) and **Justdial ₹1,214 crore** (6.3 lakh campaigns) a year (S44, S45).
4. **Compliance load.** A single-state manufacturing MSME faces **1,456 compliances a year costing ₹13–17 lakh** (S29). New labour codes (Nov-2025) and a new Income-tax Act (Apr-2026) add transition work. Compliance is mediated by **~99k CA firms**, about 150 GST filers per firm (S51).
5. **Procurement volatility and small-firm logistics penalty.**
   - **55.7%** cite raw-material price volatility (27,000-firm ISF survey; ₹124.9 lakh crore procurement economy, S31).
   - Firms under ₹5 crore pay **16.9%** of output on logistics vs **7.6%** for large firms (S40).
6. **Energy cost.** 15–40% of production cost in intensive clusters, with 10–25% savings potential (S41).
7. **EU CBAM (from 1-Jan-2026).** Default emission values 30–80% above actual emissions; 15–22% price cuts needed; orders already lost. **3–4k direct + 25–30k indirect MSME exporters exposed** (S38).

## 3. Which are commercially attractive?

Problem ≠ business. After scoring **42 problems** on 14 criteria (weighted toward willingness to pay, existing spend and distribution) and sizing **19 opportunities** top-down and bottom-up:

| Category | Opportunities | Year-5 SOM (ARR) |
|---|---|---|
| **A: strong evidence** | **O1** AI receivables & collections copilot | ₹36–72 cr |
| | **O2** AI copilot for CA firms | ₹45–75 cr |
| | **O3** AI B2B sales agent on WhatsApp | ₹48–96 cr |
| | **O4** Vertical cluster procurement + credit *(A-hard: capital- and ops-heavy)* | ₹48–96 cr net revenue |
| | **O6** CBAM/carbon compliance for MSME exporters *(A-niche: TAM ₹450–750 cr, high urgency, international upside)* | ₹15–29 cr |
| **B: promising, validate WTP** | O5 energy-as-a-service, O15 credit-readiness marketplace, O8 GeM bid desk, O12 labour-code payroll service, O9 export launchpad, O10 shop-floor digitisation, O7 incentive claims | — |
| **C: weak evidence** | Supplier quality SaaS, embedded insurance, cluster staffing | — |
| **D: avoid** | Websites/digital agencies, micro bookkeeping apps, cross-border payments, standalone cybersecurity. Big theoretical TAMs contradicted by observed revenues (Khatabook ~₹103 cr FY24; OkCredit ₹23 cr FY25) | — |

**Pattern.** MSMEs pay for **outcomes**: customers (leads), cash (credit, recovery), compliance done (CAs) and savings (energy). They do not pay for software as such. The winning models in 2026 are:
- **AI-first workflows** on **WhatsApp** in **vernacular**;
- sold through **trusted channels** (CA firms, cluster associations);
- priced on **outcomes** (success fees, savings share);
- built **on India's public infrastructure** (GST, e-invoice, AA, ULI, TReDS).

## 4. Where is the evidence strongest?

| Evidence | Opportunity |
|---|---|
| Observed spend | Leads (₹2,700+ crore, O3); procurement (OfBusiness ₹22,241 crore FY25 revenue, O4); CA software (Tally ₹500–1,000 crore; Clear ₹272 crore, O2) |
| Official measurement | Delayed payments (O1); credit gap; logistics penalty |
| Regulatory forcing functions | MSMED Amendment Act 2026 (ODR, 90-day mediation, mandatory TReDS for CPSEs) → O1; CBAM → O6; labour codes → O12 |

Weakest evidence: national OEE/quality losses, website presence, scheme-uptake barriers and state-level financing gaps are **not measured**. They are flagged as *data unavailable*.

## 5. What should be investigated next?

Two coherent theses, each with a 30-day test (chapter 13):

1. **MSME finance back-office: O2 → O1 → O15.** Win CA firms with an AI copilot, then sell receivables recovery and credit access to their clients.
2. **MSME decarbonisation stack: O6 → O5 → agri-residue biomass fuel.** Measure, then reduce, then finance. This fits AgriKhet's "Ecoharvest" identity (chapter 12).

**The 5 assumptions to validate with paid pilots before company-building** (chapter 14):

1. MSMEs or their CAs **pay for outcomes** (monthly or success fee), and not just say they would.
2. **CA firms and associations deliver customers at ≤ ₹50k CAC** and actively refer.
3. Owners **trust an AI agent** to talk to their debtors and buyers under their name.
4. **AI accuracy ≥ 85–90%** on poor-quality MSME documents and chats, at ≥ 65% gross margin.
5. **CBAM pull persists:** EU importers keep demanding verified supplier data.

**Kill rule.** If fewer than 3 of 20 target firms pay after seeing results, or CAC exceeds 12 months of gross profit, stop that opportunity (chapter 13 §13.3).

## 6. AgriKhet

AgriKhet is early-stage with no existing distribution, technology or customers, so **no track has an unfair advantage today**. The best fit of evidence and identity is **Track D (carbon/ESG): lead with O6**. The strongest pure-evidence path is **Track A/E: O1 + O2 under a separate product brand**. Run both validations, and avoid FPO-led models (Track C) until willingness to pay is shown.
