## 9.1 Problems likely to become **more** important (2026–2035)

1. **Carbon and ESG data in supply chains** (CBAM, buyer Scope-3, product passports).
2. **Receivables from private buyers.** Government-side pressure improves CPSE payment behaviour, but private-buyer discipline relies on supplier tooling and the new ODR/MSEFC routes.
3. **Customer acquisition and conversion speed**, as buyers move to chat-first, AI-assisted sourcing.
4. **Energy cost and transition** for energy-intensive clusters.
5. **Quality and traceability** for MSMEs entering export or OEM supply chains under China+1.
6. **Cybersecurity and fraud** as more money moves digitally.

## 9.2 Problems likely to **shrink or disappear** because of technology or policy

1. **Basic digital payments**: solved by UPI.
2. **Manual GST data entry and reconciliation**: shrinks with e-invoicing, IMS, auto-populated returns and AI document capture. The work shifts from "doing" to "reviewing" (good for O2 in the near term, bad for labour-intensive accounting services later).
3. **Credit underwriting cost for GST-formal MSMEs**: AA, ULI and GST cash-flow models reduce it.
4. **Scheme discovery**: shrinks as MyScheme-type portals and AI assistants make information free. What survives is *execution and claims*.
5. **Website creation**: fully commoditised by AI builders.
6. **Payments to MSMEs by CPSEs**: should improve under the mandatory TReDS rule.

**Implication.** Prefer problems where technology makes the **solution** cheaper but the **problem itself grows** (carbon data, receivables from private buyers, conversion speed, energy). Avoid problems that technology is about to remove (payments, discovery, websites, basic bookkeeping).
