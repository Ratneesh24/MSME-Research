### Lens check: which models fit which opportunity

| Model | Best fits | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **WhatsApp-first** | O1, O2, O3, O10 | MSMEs already run sales, collections and document exchange on WhatsApp (India is WhatsApp's largest market; S56, low confidence) |
| **Mobile-first** | O3, O10, O12 | Owners and supervisors are on the floor, not at desks |
| **Vernacular** | O1, O3 | The *counterparty* (debtor, buyer) speaks Hindi, Gujarati, Tamil, Punjabi and other languages |
| **AI-first** | O1, O2, O3, O6, O8 | LLMs make document understanding, conversation and report drafting cheap enough for MSME price points (new since 2024) |
| **Assisted SaaS** | O1, O6, O7, O9, O12 | Low digital maturity plus high stakes. A human closes the loop; software keeps margin |
| **Embedded finance** | O1, O4, O15 (later O3) | Money already flows through the workflow; financing earns more than SaaS |
| **B2B marketplace** | O4 only (vertical) | Horizontal marketplaces are saturated |
| **Vertical SaaS** | O2 (CA firms), O6 (exporters), O10 (job shops) | Narrow buyer, deep workflow, word-of-mouth inside communities |
| **Managed service + SaaS** | O6, O9, O12, O7 | Outcome-based buyers |
| **Agentic AI** | O1 (collections agent), O3 (sales agent), O8 (bid agent) | Tasks are repetitive, rules-bound and measurable, and the owner approves exceptions |

## 7.4 India-specific characteristics and the business models they favour

| Characteristic (evidence) | Implication | Model that wins |
|---|---|---|
| **Extreme fragmentation**: 7.92 crore establishments, 1.62 workers each (S01) | Direct sales to micro units is uneconomic | Channel-led (CAs, associations, banks), or B2B2SMB via platforms they already pay (IndiaMART, Tally) |
| **WhatsApp dependence** | The interface already exists | WhatsApp-native agents; no new app to install |
| **Multiple languages** | Buyers and debtors respond better in their own language | Vernacular AI for *outbound* conversations (O1, O3) |
| **Low SaaS willingness, high willingness to pay for outcomes**: IndiaMART ₹69k vs Khatabook monetisation struggles (S44, S47) | Owners pay for customers, cash and compliance, not for software | Success fees, outcome guarantees, bundled services |
| **CA-mediated compliance**: ~99k CA firms; ~150 GST filers per firm (S51, derived) | The CA is the trusted adviser and de facto software buyer | Sell to the CA (O2), then cross-sell through the CA (O1, O15) |
| **Trust-based, family-owned decision-making**: 64.5% of procurement decisions by owners (S31) | Long trust-building, referral-driven | Cluster density, association endorsements, peer case studies |
| **Cash-flow constraints**: 73-day collection cycle (S22) | Annual prepaid SaaS is hard to sell | Monthly billing, success fees, financing bundled with product |
| **Digital public infrastructure**: GST, e-invoice, UPI, AA, ULI, TReDS, Udyam, ONDC, GeM | Data access and rails are cheap; moats from data alone are weak | Build workflow + distribution moats on DPI rails |
| **Regulation as trigger**: 43B(h), MSMED Amendment 2026, labour codes Nov-2025, Income-tax Act 2026, CBAM Jan-2026 | Deadlines create urgency | Time launches to regulatory deadlines |
| **Preference for assisted service** | Self-serve onboarding fails | Human-in-the-loop onboarding, then automate |
