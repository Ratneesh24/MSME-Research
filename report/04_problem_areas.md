**Conflict flagged.** NITI Aayog (May 2025, S54) cites only 19% of MSME credit demand met formally by FY21 and ₹80 lakh crore unmet. SIDBI-Crisil (2025) puts the addressable gap at ₹30 lakh crore. The two use different years and definitions (total vs *addressable* demand). This study uses SIDBI-Crisil because it is newer and survey-based.

**Does this create a business?** The problem is the largest in rupee terms. **New lending is capital-intensive, regulated and crowded** (banks, FlexiLoans AUM > ₹3,000 cr, Lendingkart ₹18,700 cr cumulative, Oxyzo, Indifi, MUDRA, CGTMSE). Public infrastructure (AA, ULI, GST APIs, the PSB digital model) is lowering underwriting cost for everyone, which erodes data moats. **The startup opening is distribution and credit-readiness, not balance sheet:** loan-ready documentation, lender matching and embedded finance inside a workflow the MSME already uses (O1, O4, O15). → **B**.

### 7.2 Delayed payments and receivables: the clearest, largest operating pain

**Quantification chain**

1. **Stock of delayed receivables:**
   - **₹7.34 lakh crore** (March 2024; ~4.6% of GVA). This is down from ₹8.27 lakh crore (2023) and ₹10.7 lakh crore (2022) (GAME–FISME–C2FO *Delayed Payments 3.0*, Nov 2025, S20).
   - The **Economic Survey 2025-26 cites ~₹8.1 lakh crore** (S19). The two are consistent in order of magnitude; the difference is mainly the reference date.
2. **Who owes it:** ~**40% is owed by government agencies and PSUs** (S20).
3. **Behaviour:**
   - Indian SMEs take **73 days on average to pay invoices**, against the 45-day statutory maximum.
   - **82.6% of invoices** are issued with 0–30 day terms. The delay is a collection problem, not one of generous terms.
   - Source: Recordent *Indian SME Receivables Report 2026*, ~1.1 lakh MSMEs and 10 lakh+ transactions, S22. Its "₹3.83 crore average overdue >360 days per SME" reflects a dataset skewed to larger firms and is **not** a national average.
4. **Formal recourse is rarely used** (MSME Samadhaan to 31-Dec-2025, S21):

   | Item | Applications | Amount |
   |---|---:|---:|
   | Total filed | 2,56,892 | ₹55,244 cr (≈7% of ₹8.1 lakh cr, derived) |
   | Settled mutually | 24,238 | ₹3,018 cr |
   | Not yet examined | 52,744 | ₹8,397 cr |
   | Converted to cases | 47,088 | ₹14,243 cr |
   | Disposed by MSEFCs | 53,911 | ₹14,638 cr |
5. **Affected MSMEs (estimate):** delayed payments hit **B2B sellers**. Our estimate is **~1–1.5 crore B2B-selling units**, with the acute, monetisable core at **~8–12 lakh firms of ₹2–250 crore turnover** (chapter 3).
6. **Financial impact (derived):** at a 12–18% cost of working capital, ₹7.34–8.1 lakh crore locked up costs MSMEs **~₹0.9–1.5 lakh crore a year** in carrying cost. That excludes bad debts and lost growth. On average that is ₹60k–1.5 lakh per affected B2B firm, heavily skewed to larger suppliers. *Derived estimate; cost-of-capital range is an assumption.*

**Policy tailwinds (2023–2026)**
- **Section 43B(h)** (Finance Act 2023). Buyers lose the tax deduction for dues to micro/small suppliers unpaid beyond 15/45 days. From 1-Apr-2026 the rule is carried into **Section 37(2)(g) of the Income-tax Act 2025** (S53). *Unintended effect:* some buyers shifted to unregistered or non-MSE suppliers, or pressured suppliers to drop Udyam registration.
- **MSMED (Amendment) Act 2026** (S24):
  - mandatory **TReDS** settlement for CPSE procurement from MSMEs;
  - MSEFC mediation within **90 days**, arbitration award within 90 days of pleadings;
  - **Online Dispute Resolution**;
  - **50% deposit** of the award if a challenge is pending more than 6 months;
  - MSEFC jurisdiction based on the **supplier's** location.

  Recovery becomes faster and more productisable.
- **TReDS** discounted **₹3.47 lakh crore in FY26** (₹40,000 crore in FY22, S23). Only ~1.35 lakh MSME sellers and ~7,900 corporate buyers were registered (Mar-2025, three original platforms). In Jul-2026, RXIL had 1,03,998 sellers / 3,759 buyers and Invoicemart 68,663 / 4,496. **The constraint is buyer acceptance**, so TReDS cannot cover private-buyer and small-buyer receivables.

**Does this create a business?** **Yes. This is a Category A problem** (O1). It is large and measured, it recurs every week, the legal levers are new, and nobody owns the daily collections workflow for MSMEs. WTP for *software* is unproven, but MSMEs already pay lawyers, collection agents and TReDS discounts. A success-fee component aligns price with value.

### 7.3 Payments
90% of surveyed MSMEs accept digital payments (S17), and 82.5% of ASUSE establishments hold an account with a financial institution (S01). **Digital acceptance is largely solved by UPI.** The residual problems are reconciliation (7.4), cross-border collection (saturated; §14), and fraud (P35). → **No standalone opportunity** in domestic payments.

### 7.4 Banking, reconciliation and cash-flow visibility
Matching UPI/bank receipts to invoices and GST returns is daily work for the 1.49 crore normal GST filers. It is done by accountants and CAs in Tally and Excel. Time spent and error rates are **not measured** in any retrieved source → *data unavailable*. Cash-flow forecasting is a proven product category abroad (Agicap, $100m raised, S57), but standalone WTP in India is unproven. → **Feature inside O1/O2, not a company.**

- **Owner-facing GST/accounting software:** saturated (Tally ₹500–1,000 cr revenue; Clear ₹272 cr; Zoho; Vyapar; myBillBook; Busy; Marg) → **D for a new entrant**.
- **CA-firm productivity (O2):** A. Professional buyers already pay, the pain peaks at every due date, and AI has made document-to-ledger automation newly feasible.
- **Labour-code and factory compliance as a managed service (O12, P08):** B. Real cost (₹13–17 lakh/yr) and a regulatory trigger, but crowded.

## 10. Website and online presence

**How many MSMEs have a website?** No national measurement exists. We triangulate two ways (`data/website_presence.csv`):

| Method | Steps | Result |
|---|---|---|
| A. Domain supply | .IN ≈ 4.2 million domains (S55). Assume .IN is 35–50% of Indian-registrant domains → 8.4–12 m. Assume 30–40% host an active business site → 2.5–4.8 m. MSME share 85–90% | **21–43 lakh** |
| B. Survey | 19% of surveyed (registered-skewed) MSMEs report a website (S32/S30), applied to 1.06 crore hired-worker units (20 lakh) and 1.49 crore GST normal filers (28 lakh) | **20–28 lakh** |
| **Triangulated** | | **~20–35 lakh MSMEs (≈2.5–4.5% of ~8 crore; ≈15–25% of GST-registered MSMEs)**, low–medium confidence |

A 2026 secondary claim of "35% of MSMEs have a website" is inconsistent with both methods unless it refers to a narrow, larger-firm sample.

**Struggles.** Website creation is cheap and commoditised (AI builders, Wix, GoDaddy, Hostinger, freelancers). The real struggles are downstream: lead generation, follow-up, catalogue quality and CRM (§11).

**Does this create a business?**
- A standalone **website/digital-agency business for MSMEs is Category D**: low WTP, commoditised, diffuse CAC.
- An **"MSME digital operating system"** is only viable if anchored on a **revenue outcome**. The site, Google profile and catalogue become *inputs* to an AI sales agent that converts leads (O3). Owners pay for customers, not for web pages.

## 11. Sales and customer acquisition

**Evidence of pain and spend**
- High competition is among the top three challenges, and 70% of MSMEs rely on traditional marketing (SIDBI-Crisil 2025, S17). Only ~13% actively use digital marketing/e-commerce.
- **Observed spend on customer acquisition:**
  - IndiaMART: FY26 revenue ₹1,569 crore, collections ₹1,857 crore, **2,28,000 paying suppliers**, about **₹69k a year each** (derived) (S44). Management attributed the Q4 FY26 dip in payers to a price rise and "SME stress".
  - Justdial: ₹1,213.9 crore operating revenue, **6,31,530 paid campaigns**, about **₹19k each** (derived), 54.7 million listings (S45).
  - GeM (public procurement): FY26 GMV ₹5 lakh crore; MSMEs won **47% of order value** (₹2.36 lakh crore) and 68% of orders; 11 lakh+ MSEs registered (S34).
- **"What percentage struggle to consistently generate customers?"** No retrieved source measures this directly → *data unavailable*. Proxies: 70% rely on traditional marketing, and competition is a top challenge. **This is assumption #1 to test in interviews.**
- **CAC.** MSMEs pay roughly **₹19k–69k a year per platform** for leads. Their own cost per acquired customer is unmeasured.
- **Dependence on distributors and dealers.** Qualitatively high in FMCG, building materials, auto parts and electricals. Not quantified.

**Does this create a business?**
- **Another B2B marketplace: D.** IndiaMART's liquidity and cash are hard to match.
- **Conversion layer for MSMEs already buying leads: A** (O3). Spend is proven, the buyers are identifiable (publicly listed paid sellers), and AI agents that answer in the buyer's language within 60 seconds became feasible in 2024–26.
- **Tender/GeM bid support: B** (O8).
- **Export discovery: B** (O9).
- **Distributor management: C**, already served by DMS vendors for large brands.

- *Vertical, cluster-specific procurement with embedded credit* (O4): **A on evidence**, but capital- and ops-heavy and contested by OfBusiness and Udaan.
- *Price intelligence / forward buying*: a feature of O4.
- *Group purchasing*: a cluster-association model; it works where associations are strong.
- *Logistics aggregation for clusters*: B, with low margins.
- *Inventory financing*: via NBFC partners.
- *AI demand forecasting*: C, low WTP in micro firms.

## 15. Government schemes: why eligible MSMEs don't use them

| Scheme | Latest scale | Observed barriers |
|---|---|---|
| CGTMSE | 1.15 crore guarantees, ₹9.34 lakh cr cumulative; ceiling ₹10 cr (S25) | Fee pass-through, bank discretion, documentation |

| PMEGP | Outlay ₹13,554 cr (FY22–FY26) for ~4 lakh projects / 30 lakh jobs. FY26 achievement **not retrieved** | Bank sanction delays, DPR quality, margin-money release timing (qualitative) |
| TReDS | ₹3.47 lakh cr FY26; CPSE mandate (S23, S24) | Buyer onboarding; private buyers opt out |
| ZED / Lean | Certification counts **not verified** (portal figures retrieved were inconsistent) → *data unavailable* | Awareness; perceived value |
| MSE-CDP (clusters) | 606 projects approved, 364 completed (S59) | SPV formation, state co-funding |
| Public procurement / GeM | MSMEs 47% of FY26 GeM order value; 25% MSE target exceeded (S34) | Bid complexity, L1 pricing, payment delays |
| Export Promotion Mission | ₹25,060 cr FY26–31 (S37) | New; awareness |
| PSB digital credit model | ₹52,300 cr sanctioned Apr–Dec 2025 (S27) | Still requires clean GST/bank data |
| State incentives (capital subsidy, SGST reimbursement, power, interest) | **Not measured** nationally | Long claim cycles, documentation, opaque eligibility |

**Why eligible MSMEs don't use them (evidence):**
- **97.3%** of MSMEs surveyed by ISF were unaware of any digitalisation scheme (S30).
- 52.6% struggle to find the right tools (S30).
- 43B(h) showed that well-intended rules can **reduce** MSMEs' willingness to be visible (S53).
- Processing time, documentation and consultant dependence are consistently reported qualitatively, but **not measured** in retrieved sources.

**Could "scheme discovery + application + compliance" be a business?**
- **Discovery alone: no.** It is free on government portals, Haqdarshak already covers 6,000+ schemes, and it is a one-off need.
- **High-value incentive claims on a success fee: plausibly** (O7, B). Consultants already work this way, but market size and collection risk are unmeasured.
- **Best use:** a free scheme engine as a **lead magnet and trust builder** inside O1, O2 or O15.
